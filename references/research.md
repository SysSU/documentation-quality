# Research documents

Research artifacts should let another reader distinguish what sources show from what the author inferred.

## Adapt this structure

1. **Question and decision context** — what is being investigated and why.
2. **Scope and method** — source selection, dates, definitions, and material limits.
3. **Source evidence** — attributable findings with links or citations where available.
4. **Observations** — patterns directly visible across evidence.
5. **Interpretation** — reasoning and uncertainty.
6. **Recommendations** — proposed action, decision criteria, and confidence.
7. **Unanswered questions** — missing evidence and next research.
8. **Sources** — enough metadata to retrieve and assess each source.

## Source discipline

Prefer primary and authoritative sources for claims about products, standards, behavior, and research. Record access or publication dates when staleness matters. Do not turn search-result snippets, unsourced summaries, or model memory into evidence.

## Quality checks

- Can every consequential claim be traced to evidence or clearly identified as interpretation?
- Are conflicting sources represented fairly?
- Does the method disclose meaningful selection limits?
- Are recommendations proportional to evidence strength?
- Could another reader retrieve the cited material?
