# Project and implementation plans

A plan explains how the current state will become a desired outcome under real dependencies and uncertainty. A list of tasks is only one part of it.

## Adapt this structure

1. **Desired outcome and success evidence**
2. **Current state and gap**
3. **Scope and non-goals**
4. **Workstreams and deliverables**
5. **Dependencies and sequencing**
6. **Milestones and decision points**
7. **Owners and coordination boundaries**
8. **Risks, assumptions, and mitigations**
9. **Validation, rollout, and handoff**
10. **Open questions and change policy**

Use dates only when supported. Distinguish a target, estimate, commitment, and external deadline. Link requirements, decisions, designs, and detailed task trackers rather than restating them.

## Quality checks

- Does each workstream produce an outcome, not merely activity?
- Are dependency owners and decision points visible?
- Can progress be assessed from evidence?
- Are assumptions likely to invalidate the plan explicit?
- Does the plan say how completed work becomes owned, operated, or maintained?
