# Decision records

Use a decision record when future readers need to know what was chosen and why. Skip it for trivial, reversible choices already clear in an authoritative source.

## Minimal structure

- **Title:** a specific decision, preferably a verb phrase.
- **Status:** proposed, accepted, rejected, deprecated, or superseded.
- **Date:** decision date when known.
- **Context:** forces, constraints, and the decision that had to be made.
- **Decision:** the chosen course and scope.
- **Alternatives:** credible options considered.
- **Rationale:** criteria and evidence supporting the choice.
- **Consequences:** benefits, costs, risks, follow-up, and reversibility.
- **Supersession:** link to or from replacement decisions when applicable.

## Preserve history

Correct factual errors transparently, but do not rewrite an accepted record to make a later decision look inevitable. Add a dated note or create a superseding record. Update operational documents to describe current reality while retaining the record's historical rationale.

## Quality checks

- Is there exactly one material decision?
- Can readers tell what changed and what remains outside scope?
- Are alternatives and tradeoffs substantive?
- Is status unambiguous?
- Do current architecture or policy pages link to the controlling decision without copying its full rationale?
