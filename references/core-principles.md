# Core principles

## Optimize two systems

Evaluate every change at two levels:

1. **Local quality:** Is this document correct, complete enough, clear, structured, and usable for its intended reader?
2. **Global quality:** Is this the right home, and does the change reduce rather than create duplication, contradiction, staleness, and retrieval cost?

A locally excellent document can still damage the knowledge system by restating an authoritative policy or hiding a decision in an unexpected place.

## Establish the contract

Before substantial writing, identify:

- **Purpose:** Why must this artifact exist?
- **Audience:** Who will use it, and what do they already know?
- **Reader outcome:** What will they understand, decide, or accomplish?
- **Scope:** What belongs here, and what does not?
- **Evidence:** Which supplied materials, observed behavior, research, or authoritative documents support it?
- **Lifecycle:** Is it a durable reference, active plan, point-in-time record, or temporary note?

If the answer is unavailable after inspecting existing context, label the gap. Do not silently choose one.

## Label epistemic status

Use wording or lightweight labels that keep unlike claims separate:

- **Fact:** supported by an authoritative source or verified behavior.
- **Decision:** an approved choice; include status/date when lifecycle matters.
- **Assumption:** treated as true for now but not verified.
- **Recommendation:** proposed action or judgment.
- **Hypothesis:** testable explanation awaiting evidence.
- **Unknown / open question / TBD:** unresolved; name the needed source or owner when known.

Avoid passive certainty such as “the system uses…” when the only support is an unverified note.

## Gather context proportionately

Inspect what is available and material: related documents, tickets, requirements, conversations, prior decisions, source behavior, tests, research, linked pages, and change history. The riskier or more authoritative the artifact, the stronger the verification burden.

Do not convert a lack of evidence into extra prose. A precise unknown is higher quality than a plausible invention.

## Write for retrieval and maintenance

- Put the answer where a future reader would search for it.
- Name files and headings with stable domain language and likely search terms.
- Keep overview, task guidance, reference facts, and rationale distinct when mixing them would confuse intent.
- Prefer durable statements; qualify time-sensitive statements with status, date, or verification point when useful.
- Link to the authoritative source at the point of need.
- Remove text that does not help the intended reader act or decide.
